Global silver demand exceeded mine supply by an estimated **215 million troy ounces** in 2025, marking the fourth consecutive year of structural deficit. This report examines the components of that deficit, the above-ground stock drawdown required to cover it, and what supply-demand dynamics suggest for 2026.

<Callout type="note">
All figures are preliminary estimates based on publicly available data from the Silver Institute, USGS, and major mining company filings as of January 2026. Final annual data will be published by the Silver Institute in Q2 2026.
</Callout>

## Demand: Three Engines Running Simultaneously

### Industrial Demand

Industrial applications consumed an estimated **710 Moz** in 2025, up 8% year-over-year. Three categories drove outsized growth:

- **Solar (PV)**: Silver paste consumption in photovoltaic cells accounted for approximately 220 Moz — a 19% increase driven by record solar installations globally. Thrifting efforts by panel manufacturers have partially offset capacity growth but have not reversed the absolute volume trend.
- **EVs & Charging Infrastructure**: Electric vehicle production reached 22 million units globally in 2025. Silver content per vehicle (contacts, relays, thermal management) averages 25–50g. Infrastructure build-out (charging stations, grid upgrades) added a further estimated 18 Moz.
- **AI Data Centers**: The rapid buildout of AI compute infrastructure contributed an estimated 30 Moz through high-reliability contacts, MLCC components, and thermal interface materials.

### Physical Investment

Retail coin and bar demand remained elevated at approximately **310 Moz**, consistent with 2024. Demand was particularly strong in North America and Europe, where sovereign mint premiums remained compressed through dealer channel competition.

ETF holdings declined modestly (net -12 Moz) as some institutional investors rotated toward gold following the silver price rally in Q3.

### Jewelry and Silverware

Combined jewelry and silverware demand came in at approximately **195 Moz**, roughly flat year-over-year.

## Supply: Constrained and Concentrated

### Mine Production

Global silver mine output is estimated at **850 Moz** in 2025, a marginal increase of 1.2% from 2024. Production remains geographically concentrated:

| Region | Share of Production |
|--------|-------------------|
| Mexico | 23% |
| Peru | 15% |
| China | 13% |
| Russia | 7% |
| Chile | 6% |
| Other | 36% |

New project development has been insufficient to replace depleting reserves at existing operations. Average silver grades at primary silver mines have declined from ~350 g/t in 2015 to approximately ~270 g/t in 2025.

### Secondary Supply (Recycling)

Scrap and recycling contributed approximately **180 Moz**, slightly above 2024 levels as higher prices incentivized recovery from industrial scrap streams. Photographic recycling continued its structural decline.

### Total Supply: ~1,030 Moz

Against total demand of approximately **1,245 Moz**, the implied deficit is **~215 Moz**.

## Above-Ground Stocks

The gap between mine supply and demand has been covered by drawdowns of above-ground stocks — primarily ETF inventories, LBMA/COMEX registered stocks, and opaque private holdings.

LBMA-reported vaulted silver declined from approximately 900 Moz at the start of 2025 to an estimated 730 Moz by year-end — a drawdown of 170 Moz, consistent with (but not fully accounting for) the deficit figure above.

<Callout type="warning">
Above-ground stock estimates carry significant uncertainty. A material portion of global silver stocks are held in private hands and are not reported in any public dataset.
</Callout>

## 2026 Outlook

The structural drivers of demand — solar, EVs, AI infrastructure — are not expected to reverse in 2026. On the supply side, no major new primary silver mines are scheduled to come online in the next 18 months.

Key variables to watch:

1. **Solar installation rate**: Any slowdown in global solar buildout would have an outsized impact on industrial demand.
2. **EV penetration curve**: Battery chemistry shifts (notably the move toward solid-state batteries) could alter silver content per vehicle.
3. **Chinese industrial activity**: China accounts for ~13% of mine supply and a large share of industrial demand; domestic policy and economic conditions matter on both sides.
4. **ETF flows**: Renewed institutional accumulation could accelerate stock drawdowns; significant dishoarding would provide relief.

Our base case is a continued but modestly narrowing deficit of approximately **180–200 Moz** in 2026, with price implications dependent heavily on how financial markets respond to the physical supply picture.
